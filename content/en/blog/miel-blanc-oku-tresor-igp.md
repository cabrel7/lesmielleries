On the North-West highlands, around Mount Oku, beekeepers harvest a honey unlike any other: **a white honey**.

## A high-altitude terroir

The Oku area is a land of mountains and forests, far from cities and industry. Bees forage on high-altitude flora that gives the honey its ivory colour and delicate taste.

## A Protected Geographical Indication

Oku white honey is harvested in a village with **PGI status** and an approved specification. This recognition protects:

- **consumers**, who are guaranteed an origin and know-how;
- **beekeepers**, whose work is valued and protected against imitations.

## Why is it white and creamy?

A honey's colour and texture depend on the flowers bees visit. Oku honey **crystallises finely and naturally**, giving it a creamy, almost buttery consistency. It isn't a defect or a sign of additives: it's its signature.

## How to enjoy it

- Straight from the spoon, to savour its mildness;
- on warm bread or toast;
- with yoghurt or fresh cheese;
- as a gift: it's an exceptional product.
