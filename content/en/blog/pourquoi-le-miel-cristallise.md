You open your jar and the once-runny honey has become thick and grainy. Should you worry? **No.**

## A 100% natural process

Honey is mainly made of two sugars: **glucose** and **fructose**. Over time, glucose tends to form crystals, especially in cool conditions. The more glucose a honey contains, the faster it crystallises.

That's why some honeys stay liquid for months, while others, like [Oku white honey](/en/products/miel-blanc-oku), crystallise quickly and finely.

## Is it a sign of poor quality?

Quite the opposite: crystallisation shows the honey **has not been heated or processed**. A heavily heated industrial honey can stay liquid for a long time, but at the cost of some of its qualities.

## How to make it runny again

1. Place the jar in a **warm water bath** (around 40 °C maximum).
2. Stir gently until the crystals melt.
3. Avoid the microwave and boiling water, which damage honey.

## Storing honey properly

- At room temperature, between 15 and 25 °C;
- away from light and moisture;
- jar tightly closed, clean and dry spoon.

Stored well, honey keeps for a very long time.
