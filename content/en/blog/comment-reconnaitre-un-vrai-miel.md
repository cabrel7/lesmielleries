Honey is one of the most frequently adulterated foods in the world. Added sugar syrup, overheated honey, misleading origins: the tricks are many. How can you find your way?

## Home tests: handle with care

You've probably heard of these tricks:

- **The glass of water**: real honey supposedly sinks without dissolving.
- **The match**: a match dipped in real honey would still light.
- **The thumb**: a drop of real honey would stay in place on your finger.

They're fun, but **none of them is reliable**. A thick syrup can pass the water test, and a naturally moist honey can fail the match test. None of them replaces a lab analysis.

## What really matters

### 1. Traceability

A serious producer can tell you **where the honey comes from**: region, flowers, beekeepers. At Les Mielleries, our honeys come from Cameroon's three main beekeeping regions — the savannah of the North and Adamawa, and the mountains and forests of the West and North-West — from identified producers.

### 2. The label

Read it carefully: product name, origin, net weight, packer details, best-before date. A "honey" with no clear origin should make you wary.

### 3. Laboratory analysis

It's the only truly reliable method. Among other things, it measures water content, sugars and the pollen present, which reveals the floral origin.

## Crystallisation: a good sign

Contrary to popular belief, **honey that crystallises is not sugared honey**. It's a natural process that depends on the honey's sugar composition. Our [Oku white honey](/en/products/miel-blanc-oku) is a fine example, with its creamy texture.

## In short

Trust a transparent producer, read labels, and be wary of suspiciously low prices. Real honey has a cost: the work of bees and beekeepers.
